GVT STUDIO r43 — the battle-scene recorder (Green vs. Tan)
For the team: Wyatt, Isaac, Dylan, Al, Laura, Skylar. Mac only. Needs Google Chrome installed.

GET GOING
1. Unzip "GVT Studio". Keep the folder together (the app, studio/, autoplay/).
2. First time: macOS asks once because the app is unsigned. Double-click "GVT Studio.app"; when macOS says it could not
   verify it, click Done, then System Settings → Privacy & Security → scroll to Security → Open Anyway → Open.
   (macOS 14 or older: right-click the app → Open → Open.) In a hurry, one Terminal line, then double-click:
   xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Downloads/"GVT Studio"
   macOS may also ask once to let GVT Studio control System Events: allow it (that is how it brings its window forward).
3. The studio opens on the battleground at THE CROSSING, paused, with the units board (the palette) open.

THE LOOP
- Drag men from the palette onto the field: yours on the left, the computer's on the right. Or TAP a tile (press and let go without dragging): he lands on a random clear spot of his own side of the map, yours on your side and the computer's on theirs. That works for every tile of the units board: men, heroes, Sarges, planes and the enemy casts. Tap the same tile again and again to fill a side fast. Press on a man on the field and drag him anywhere. Double-press a man to pin the film camera to him (a red dot while paused; double-press again to let go). PIN X on the controls sets where the pinned camera aims: 0 = right on him, 50 (the start) = a quarter-frame ahead to his right, 100 = he stands on the frame's left edge; negatives lead to the left. Type a number, hold − / +, or press , and . to nudge by 5.
- Planes: the AIR row on the palette. Drop the V2 BOMBER where its bombs should fall: when you PLAY it flies in from its side's edge, drops its stick across that spot, turns at the far edge and comes back for a second stick (while paused a dashed line marks its run). Drop a DOGFIGHTER or the V2 DOGFIGHTER where it should take off: it climbs, patrols over the other side's half, strafes their men and fights their planes. On a battleground both sides fly them; in a campaign level, a boss or a park only yours. They fly at the palette's UNIT LEVEL; UNDO and CLEAR THE FIELD take them off like everything else.
- ENEMY CASTS (on BOTH columns of the palette, under the Sarges): six tabs, ALL · DINO · YETI · BEAR · GNOME · OUTLAW. ALL (the start) shows every cast at once, each under its name; a cast's own tab shows that cast alone. The tiles are the game's own enemies: the little dinos, the triceratops, the pterodactyl and King Rex; the munchkin yetis, the snowmen, the cave yetis and King Yeti; Private Snugg, the cub scouts, the bluecoats and Emperor Bear; the gnomes, the wasps, the fairy and the Queen Wasp; the outlaws, the riders, the rattlesnake, Buffalo Bill and Madam Cathrine. Drag one onto the field like a man: it walks, fights and falls as it does in the game, and your men fight it. They work on every level: a battleground, a campaign level, a boss fight or a park. Drag one from the computer's column and it fights for the computer; drag one from YOUR column and it fights for you: Yetis against Dinos, bears against outlaws, any cast against any other. Every cast member, on every level and for either army, is the game's own enemy and behaves as it does in its park: the snowman throws snowballs, Sergeant Snowman and the Giant Snowman roll their boulders, the triceratops rams, the bruiser stomps, the egg hatches, the nest sends out wasps, the kings fight their own fights (King Yeti calls his helpers, King Rex breathes fire). They go for the other army's men, heroes, Sarges and cast members, and the other army goes for them. On a battleground their health and their hits follow the palette's UNIT LEVEL, like the men's. A few never attack, as in their parks: the Fairy heals, the Honor Guard escorts, Sarge Gruffle rallies, the Outlaw Mouse sneaks; the cave yetis and the Ferocious Yeti are built for the Ice Caves and do little outside them. A tile's tip says what is special about it (an egg hatches, a nest sends out wasps, Mr. Buttons waits). UNDO, CLEAR THE FIELD, ERASE, HEALTH, the camera's PIN and the CAMERA SHOTS work on them as on any man. Keep CPU HOLD on and the level sends nobody of its own: no waves, and no more squads of tan riflemen dropping in by their crate when your side is winning (r38 let those through).
- PLACE ITEMS (I, on the SCENE row under RECORD) opens a palette of the game's own pieces by kind: COVER AND TERRAIN, TRAPS, BUFFS AND ABILITIES and the rest the game has. Drag one onto the field like a man: the men take cover behind it, trip it or pick it up as in a real fight. A dimmed piece does not work on this field (its note says why).
- EVENTS (E) opens the events page: the weather and the sky, the winter rooftop and the other places' events (not the house and room ones). Pick one to add it to the scene; AT n s fires it n seconds into the take (R starts the clock; with nothing recording, n seconds after PLAY); 0 = the moment the take starts. ▶ tries one at once. The scene's events show as chips on the SCENE row.
- ERASE (X) is a mode: while it is on, press any man, plane or item on the field (a red ring shows what goes) or an event chip, and it is out of the scene: nothing dies, it is lifted off. Press ERASE again (or X, or Esc) to go back to normal. UNDO still puts the whole field back; CLEAR THE FIELD also clears the items you placed and stops an event left running.
- CAMERA SHOTS (K, on the SHOTS row under the SCENE row) is the camera's timeline. ADD its steps: ZOOM IN (the camera moves in on a spot to the zoom you set, 1× = the take's own framing up to 4×), HOLD (it stays put: room for a voice-over), PAN (it travels to another spot, keeping its zoom or changing it on the way), RESET (back to the take's own framing as it was before the first shot, or to a spot and zoom you choose). PICK sets a step's spot: press the field; press a man and the camera follows him. Each step has AT (seconds into the take; empty = right when the step before it ends) and FOR (how long its move takes), and every move eases in and out. Add as many as a clip needs, on any men. The steps play by themselves the moment R starts a take, the same on every retake; with nothing recording, the moment PLAY runs the fight. ▶ PREVIEW (W) plays them from the set-up without recording. The zoomed picture is drawn at full sharpness, and every FILES mode records it.
- BATTLE STARTS (on the SHOTS row): WITH PLAY (as before), AT n s (on the take's clock), or AFTER THE SHOTS. Until it fires everyone stands in place, both armies: no march, no shots, the computer and the planes wait. The weather, the fires, the smoke and your events go on. With a timer set, R on a paused set-up runs the field at once and the battle starts on time; a take that stops first leaves the field paused.
- HEALTH (Y) is a mode: press any man on the field (yours or theirs, a plain troop, a Sarge, a boss) and type his health, an exact number, with his normal maximum shown. Lower than that and he starts wounded, higher and he is tougher than normal: so you decide how long a fight runs and who wins. A man whose health you set wears his number on your screen while paused (never in the take). UNDO keeps it.
- Drag empty ground to move your view; VIEW + / − zooms it; WHOLE shows the whole world. Drag inside the white finder to move the film camera.
- PLAY (Space) runs the fight; pause again any time. UNDO (Backspace) puts the whole field back exactly as it stood at the first PLAY: the men, every explosion and scorch mark, every wreck and shot, the purse and the clock.
- R records the take through Chrome's hardware H.264 encoder (24 Mbps, the same picture as before): up to 60 frames a second on an Apple-chip Mac, 30 on an older Intel Mac. FILES on the SCENE row says what a take records; press it to cycle: TWO FILES (the 9:16 portrait and the 16:9 landscape, two files) → PORTRAIT ONLY → LANDSCAPE ONLY → back to TWO FILES. It starts on TWO FILES on an Apple-chip Mac and on PORTRAIT ONLY on an Intel one, where one file costs about half what two cost; your choice is kept.
- When you stop (R again) the studio shows the take and asks: SAVE TO TAKES FOLDER (S) or DELETE (X). Only a take you save lands in studio/takes/; nothing is kept by itself. A take left unanswered (the window closed) waits in studio/pending/ and is asked about again the next time the studio opens. REVEAL LAST TAKE shows the last take you saved.
- ▼ HIDE CONTROLS folds the tools; the palette stays; ▲ SHOW CONTROLS brings them back. The controls are as tall as their rows now (up to about three quarters of the window; past that they scroll).
- Another level: BATTLEGROUND / CAMPAIGN LEVEL / a boss / a park in the door row, then GO. CLEAR THE FIELD empties both sides.
- The YOU and CPU colour menus on the palette set each army's colour.

NOTES
- This is GVT Studio r43 (11/11/2026): THE CASTS BEHAVE AS IN THEIR PARKS (their own weapons and moves, for either army); TAP a tile and he lands on a random spot of his own side; and r41's ENEMY CASTS on the palette (Dino, Yeti, Bear, Gnome, Outlaw), on every level and for EITHER army, with ALL to see every cast at once. On top of r38 (Skylar's CAMERA SHOTS on a timeline, the BATTLE STARTS timer, HEALTH for any man), r37 (PLACE ITEMS, EVENTS with their timers, SAVE OR DELETE after a take, the FILES cycle, ERASE, the SCENE row), r36 (the V2 Bomber and the Dogfighters on the palette) and the smooth-on-older-Macs changes.
- The game inside is a frozen copy (the version is in the window title's staged line). Nothing you do here touches the live game or any account.
- No ffmpeg on your Mac? Takes are still saved (Chrome's own mp4).
- Smooth on older Macs (Skylar's changes, tested on his 2015 MacBook Pro: takes went from 7-11 to 28 frames a second). On an Intel Mac your screen shows the field a little lighter while nothing records, at most 30 times a second, and while a take rolls the field outside the white finder refreshes a strip at a time (Q switches that on or off on any Mac). Only your screen changes: the take is drawn in full. Paused with your hands off, the studio redraws 12 times a second on every Mac. On an older Mac keep UI ON SCREEN (G) off while recording: the game's top bar and tray cost about two thirds of the frames there.
- If Chrome quits in the middle of a take, that take is lost (the file is finished when you stop).
- Trouble: quit Chrome windows named GVT STUDIO and click the app again. The app's log is /tmp/gvt-studio-app.log.
